British English Practice – A Cup of Tea

Welcome to activities for practicing British English! This page is connected with my previous post: British English: What Textbooks Won't Tell You, so if you haven't read it, have a look at it first! Then, come back to these activities to practice your skills.

Have you read it? Good, then let's begin!

First off, what is the most important characteristic of British speech?

The answer: (click here) Politeness.

What phrases can we use to show it? For example:

  • "Could you open the window please?"
  • "Would you tell me what happened at the party?"
  • Add your own examples!

Are you familiar with these phrases? First, try to answer the questions yourself, click the text to see the answers.

  • What does it mean when someone asks you how you "take" your tea? (click here) "how do you want it?" (with or without sugar, milk, etc.)
  • What is a "cuppa?" (Answer: (click here) a cup of tea, specifically.)
    • What does it mean "to fancy" something? (Answer: (click here) to like.)

Now, watch the video and listen to this conversation. Make notes:

  • What is the tone of the conversation?
  • What kind of language is used? (formal or informal)
  • How well do the people in the video know each other? How can you tell?

Speaking time! How would you:

  • Offer a guest a cup of coffee?
  • Comment on someone's cooking? (Politely!)
  • Ask for a second serving of dessert?

Pick any of these prompts and write out your answer!

That will be all, happy learning!
